# Testing and iterating

The game's rules live in the OpenKingdoms engine, which Unity loads as `okengine`. This page covers the Unity side: running it, testing it and where things go.

## One-time setup

1. Open Unity Hub from the Start menu and sign in with your Unity account. Under Preferences, then Licenses, add a Unity Personal license.
2. In Hub, go to Installs, then Locate, and pick `D:\Unity\6000.3.25f1\Editor\Unity.exe`.
3. In Hub, go to Projects, then Add, then Add project from disk, and pick `C:\Projects\openkingdoms-unity\unity`. The first open imports for a few minutes.

## Running the tests

Close the Unity editor on this project, then run:

```
powershell -File scripts\unity-test.ps1
```

That runs the EditMode and PlayMode tests headless and prints how many passed. It needs the Unity license from the setup above. Without one Unity exits with code 198.

On Linux, as in a cloud session, `bash scripts/cloud-unity-test.sh` runs the same suites on the real engine. It fetches the Linux editor the project names into `~/unity` and takes a Unity Personal seat with `UNITY_EMAIL` and `UNITY_PASSWORD`, builds `libokengine.so` from OpenKingdoms' `unity-embed` branch in `~/OpenKingdoms`, runs its `test_embed`, and puts it in `Assets/Plugins/x86_64`, where the installer leaves a build of the right version alone. The game files come from `~/takdata`, fetched from `OK_GAME_TAR_URL` and `OK_DATA_TAR_URL` when it holds none, and `OK_GAME_DIR` and `OK_DATA_DIR` point elsewhere. Each step is skipped when its result is already there. The editor draws through Mesa on a virtual display, and the results XML, the logs and a summary with every failure and skip go to `~/unity-test`. A seat the script took is returned at the end unless `OKU_KEEP_SEAT=1`.

Without a license, `bash scripts/csharp-check.sh` from Git Bash gets part of the way. It compiles every script in `unity/Assets/Scripts` and `unity/Assets/Engine` against the editor's own UnityEngine assemblies, then runs the formation drag's pure tests in plain .NET. It uses the compiler and runtime inside the Unity install, so nothing else needs installing. It does not replace a real Unity run, because Unity's Mono and the editor itself are not involved.

Press Play in any scene, even an empty one, and the engine's own view of a real map comes up when `okengine.dll` and your game files are there. When they are not, the screen says what is missing and what to do, such as restarting Unity after an engine update or picking your game folder in OpenKingdoms, then Settings.

Changes to C# scripts only (`unity/Assets/Scripts`) do not need a restart. Unity recompiles when you switch back to it.

## Playing the remaster

Pick OpenKingdoms, then Play Remaster. The game starts at the main menu, runs on the real engine when `okengine.dll` and your game files are there, and on the mock engine otherwise. Skirmish sets up the map, your kingdom, the computer seats and the options. F1, the Pause key or the Menu button opens the game menu, which saves the game, and F2 opens the options over the battle. Load on the skirmish screen brings a save back.

In a battle the controls are the original's by default. A left click selects a unit, orders the selection to the ground or at an enemy, or carries out an armed command. A right click or Escape cancels an armed command, or else deselects, and never pauses. Drag a box to select, and hold Shift to add. M, P and G arm move, patrol and guard, Ctrl A arms attack, and Ctrl S stops. Hold Shift while giving an order to queue it after the ones the selection already has. While Shift is down, a line runs from each selected unit through every order it has queued, and from a factory to its rally point. With a factory selected, a left click on the ground sets its rally point. Ctrl with a digit makes a group, and the digit alone brings it back. Options, then Controls, switches to a modern scheme where the right button gives orders. The camera pans with WASD, the arrow keys, the screen edge or a Shift middle drag, and zooms with the wheel. A middle drag up and down tilts it and raises or lowers it together, and left and right turns it. Q and E also turn it, Page Up and Page Down tilt it, and Home returns to the classic view.

With units selected, hold the order button on the ground and drag to set them out in a formation, the way Total War sets units in battle. The order button is the right one in the modern scheme. In the classic scheme it is Ctrl with the left button, or the right button held for a fifth of a second, so a quick right click still cancels however far the hand moves. The line you draw is the front rank and the direction of the drag sets the facing, left to right facing away from the camera. A drag shorter than two cells sets no direction. The formation then faces the way the camera looks and stands centred on the press, as a click would. Melee stands in front, archers behind, cavalry on the wings, and the monarch at the back. A line is never more than four ranks deep, and a unit wider than the rest of its group, such as a catapult or a god, stands in a rank of its own behind so the others keep their spacing. Tab changes the shape between Line, Block, Wedge and Loose, F turns it about, Alt keeps the group's current layout, and Shift at the release queues it. Each role block marches at the pace of its slowest unit, so the knights are not held back to the catapults, and G lets every unit keep its own pace for that order. Escape or the other button lets the drag go. A press on an enemy still attacks it, and a short press still does what a click did before.

In the Unity editor, keys reach the game only while the Game view has focus. With Play Focused chosen in the Game view's toolbar it has focus as soon as Play starts. After clicking in another editor window, click the Game view once to give the keys back. That click may be taken as focus rather than as a click in the game.

The battle screen keeps the original's layout. A sidebar on the right holds the minimap at the top, the Menu button and the clock, the orders, spells and stances in the original's places, a help box, and the crystal ball with the mana pool, income on its left and spending on its right. A strip along the bottom shows the selected unit, its health, its mana and what it is doing, and at its right end the unit under the pointer or the one the selection is after. Hovering a bar shows its numbers in the help box. With the original's controls a left click on the minimap sends the selected units there, or moves the view there when nothing of yours is selected, and a right click moves the view. With the modern controls the left button moves the view and the right sends the selection. Everything a selected builder or factory can make shows at once in a row above the strip. A builder's card shows a ghost of the building, green where it can stand. A factory's card queues a unit and Shift queues five. A right click takes one off, or five with Shift. Ctrl makes the factory build that unit over and over, shown as +++ on the card, until a right click on the card stops it. A factory still being built can be selected only to queue units, and it starts on them once it is finished. The menus and the sidebar play the original's own button sounds from your game files. Options, then Interface size, scales the panels from 60 to 130 percent, 100 being the original at 640 by 480, and Key letters hides the key on each button. The world camera draws only the play area beside and above the panels.

## Pictures and clips of what you see

Windows Game Bar doesn't open over the Unity editor, so the game and the studio take their own. F9 saves a PNG of the Game view, the game only and not the editor around it, and Shift+F9 records about five seconds at 12 frames a second, with a contact sheet that puts every frame in one picture with its number and time. They go to `D:\OKBuild\owner-shots` when `D:\OKBuild` exists and to `Captures` at the top of the clone otherwise, and `LATEST.txt` there names the newest, so whoever looks next can open it straight away. OpenKingdoms, then Settings, changes the folder for Play and the studio, and the `OKU_SHOTS_DIR` environment variable wins over that. A built copy of the game doesn't see the setting, so set `OKU_SHOTS_DIR` for it when the default won't do. The same keys work in the Studio View and the Studio Gallery, and docs/STUDIO_MODE.md has the details.

F12 was the first choice, but the original's Keys.tdf gives it to ClearChat and keeps F9 free for screenshots, and the remaster's own keys leave F9 alone. The capture reads the screen back asynchronously and writes the files on a worker thread, so it hardly holds the game up. The first picture after pressing Play reads the screen once more the slow way to learn which way up it comes back, and at 4K a clip's frames take the worker longer to write than they take to arrive, so the last of them land a few seconds after the clip.

## Testing the remaster

The EditMode tests cover the screen flow, the battle HUD's layout at every screen size and interface size, the mock engine, the terrain builder, the override rules, the glb reader, the studio windows, Studio Mode, the engine installer, the capture folder and file names, and the clip's contact sheet. The Studio Gallery's tests check the grid for overlaps and spacing by footprint, the search, the sizes read from a file's header against the loader's, hot reload, the originals beside each model, loading nearest first within the memory budget, and a folder of 800 models that fails on any editor frame over a second, both when it opens and when every file in it is written again at once. A folder picked with forward slashes finds the gallery already open on it. The PlayMode tests go from the main menu through skirmish setup and the loading screen into a running game and back, save and load a game, play a fight to its end, and time five hundred units on screen. The capture tests take a picture in a running game and check its size, which way up it is and that the note about the last capture is not in it, record a clip and count its sixty frames and its sheet, cut a clip short and find its sheet, press F9 and Shift+F9 through the same path the keys take, and follow LATEST.txt. In batch mode there is no screen, so they draw the cameras again. Run with a window, they read the screen itself as the owner's F9 does and check that they did, and that run opens a Unity window for a minute: `Unity.exe -projectPath unity -runTests -testPlatform PlayMode -testFilter OpenKingdomsUnity.Tests.CapturePlayTests` without `-batchmode`. All of them run on the mock engine, so they need no game files.

The capture test draws every screen into PNG files without a window. It runs only when `OKU_CAPTURE_DIR` names a folder. Set `OKU_CAPTURE_BACKEND=engine` to use the real engine, `OKU_CAPTURE_MAP` to pick a map by name, and `OKU_CAPTURE_W` and `OKU_CAPTURE_H` for another size than 1920 by 1080. With `OKU_CAPTURE_TREES=1` it also frames the thickest stand of trees with the fog and weather off. The water capture `WaterCaptures` runs when `OKU_WATER_MAPS` also names maps, split by `;`. For each it shoots the open sea, a coast close up, a low view toward the sun, a wide one and a coast at the map's edge where one runs on into the ring, in clear weather, rain and fog. A name ending in `+fow` adds the fog of war at the start with the map revealed, so water out of sight shows as seen before, then walks the start's units to the shore so their sight lies over the water. One ending in `+ships` builds a Veruna harbour and three ships, shoots them at rest, low from the side and in three frames a little apart to show them bob, and sails them out. With `OKU_CAPTURE_PIPELINE=builtin` it draws in the built-in pipeline instead of URP, and the file names say which. With `OKU_CAPTURE_SEAPORT=1`, `SeaportCaptures` has the first seat's monarch raise a building on each map in `OKU_CAPTURE_MAP`, split by `;`, and shoots it and the longest sea edge, or the west edge where there is none, from the classic camera and a low three-quarter view. With `OKU_CAPTURE_HUD=1` and the engine, `HudCaptures` draws the battle HUD for each kingdom's monarch and for the building it raises that builds the most, at 1280 by 720, 1920 by 1080 and 3840 by 2160, then the first kingdom's monarch at 130 percent and with line of sight on, and `OKU_CAPTURE_SIDES` limits it to some kingdoms. The pictures in `docs/images/studio-mode` come from the EditMode test `CapturesForTheGuide` on the mock, which runs only when `OKU_STUDIO_SHOTS` names a folder. Pictures of the formation drag in each shape come from the PlayMode test `FormationCaptures`, which runs only when `OKU_FORMATION_SHOTS` names a folder.

The effects have their own tests. `EffectTests` in EditMode checks the original's rules for the retail art (which frames add light and which alpha blend, and how long each frame shows), the beams' colours and jag, that beams and straight shots point the way they fly, that the engine's records are played at the original's pace even when they are read only every third tick, that a moving sprite keeps the engine's own frame, and that every weapon family the mock fires, from arrows and cannon balls to beams, flame breath, rings, rain and a whirlwind, comes out as a drawing. It also checks the remaster's parts: only a weapon with a lightmap lights the ground, as a soft glow in its own colour, and a builder's sparkle lights nothing, a beam cut short at its source draws no ball of light, flames hold each frame while a still blast eases, only fire leaves a trail and never one longer than the shot, and in a crowd of blasts the lights hold their places and fade rather than blink. `FxPlayTests` in PlayMode stages each reference scene on the mock and checks what it draws, checks that added art lands on the screen as the ground plus the art's own bytes, as the original adds them, that an enemy's fire out of sight draws, lights and scorches nothing until a unit of ours sees it, and keeps more than two hundred effects on screen while it times the effects' own work and counts their garbage. `FxCaptures` saves pictures of the same scenes when `OKU_FX_DIR` names a folder. It stages them on the mock, or with `OKU_CAPTURE_BACKEND=engine` on an engine built with the effects stage, which plays them when `OK_FXSTAGE` names a scene. `OKU_FX_SCENES` picks scenes from magic, magic2, ranged, ground, fire, rings, spells and bolts, `OK_FXSHOTS` the ticks, `OKU_FX_LOOK=original` turns off the remaster's parts to show the original's look alone, and `OKU_FX_DUMP=1` lists every effect and shot in each scene's log.

Some of the look waits on the engine. okx API 20 reports no caster's nimbus, so no spell's caster glows yet. It reports no frame's size or anchor until that frame has been seen, no ring's own pace (the original spreads a ring sprite's frames once over the ring's life), and a beam's source only as the ground under its shooter. On the API 20 engine some shots burst on or beside their own shooter, which the classic client on the same engine shows too.

## Where things go

- Rules, anything that decides what happens: the OpenKingdoms engine, published here as `engine/okengine-api<N>.dll`. `engine/README.md` covers publishing a new build.
- The engine binding: `unity/Assets/Engine`. `OkEngine.cs` declares the engine's calls and `EngineBackend.cs` offers them to the game through `IGameBackend`.
- Anything you see or hear: `unity/Assets/Scripts/Game`, in C#. `GameRoot` runs the game and its screens, `World` draws the battle and `UI` holds the menus and the HUD.
- Models: `unity/Assets/Overrides/Units` and `unity/Assets/Overrides/Features`, as glb, gltf, fbx or prefab. `docs/STUDIO.md` covers names, scale and facing.
